1. ⚡ MAGNETIC FORCE

Definition

Magnetic force is the force exerted by a magnetic field on a moving charged particle or a current-carrying conductor.

Force on a Moving Charge

F = qvB sinθ

Where q is charge, v is velocity, B is magnetic field and θ is the angle between velocity and magnetic field.

Vector Form

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗)
B ↑ │ │ ●────────→ v / / F F is perpendicular to v and B

Special Cases

1. θ = 90°

F = qvB

The force is maximum.

2. θ = 0°

F = 0

The particle is moving parallel to the field.

3. θ = 180°

F = 0

The particle is moving opposite to the field.

Direction

For a positive charge, use the right-hand rule. For a negative charge, the direction is opposite.

Worked Example

A charge of 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C moves at 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 1.5 T magnetic field. Find the force.

F = qvB

F = (3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2 × 10⁶)(1.5)

F = 9.6 × 10⁻¹³ N

Important Facts

  • Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity.
  • A magnetic field does no work on a moving charged particle.
  • Force increases when q, v or B increases.
  • Maximum force occurs at 90°.
Common mistake: Do not forget sinθ. Use F = qvB only when the motion is perpendicular to the magnetic field.
Exam Practice
  1. Define magnetic force.
  2. State the magnetic force equation.
  3. When is magnetic force maximum?
  4. When is magnetic force zero?
  5. Calculate the force on a moving charge.
  6. Explain the direction of force on a negative charge.

2. 🧲 MAGNETIC FIELD

Definition

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet, current-carrying conductor, or moving charge where magnetic effects can be experienced.

Unit of magnetic field = Tesla (T)

Magnetic Field Lines

→ → → → → → → ↗ ↘ ↗ ↘ N S ↘ ↗ ↘ ↗ → → → → → → → BAR MAGNET

Properties

  • Outside a magnet, lines go from N to S.
  • They form closed loops.
  • They never cross.
  • Closer lines indicate stronger fields.
  • The tangent gives field direction.

Field Due to a Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

The field increases with current and decreases with distance.

μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Worked Example

A wire carries 10 A. Find B at 0.20 m.

B = μ₀I / 2πr

B = [(4π × 10⁻⁷)(10)] / [(2π)(0.20)]

B = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ T
Remember: Doubling I doubles B. Doubling r halves B.
Exam Practice
  1. Define magnetic field.
  2. State its SI unit.
  3. List five properties of magnetic field lines.
  4. Derive B = μ₀I/2πr.
  5. Calculate the field around a straight wire.

3. 🧮 BIOT–SAVART LAW

Meaning

The Biot–Savart law determines the magnetic field contribution produced by a small current element.

dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl sinθ/r²)

Vector Form

dB⃗ = (μ₀/4π)[I(dl⃗ × r̂)/r²]

Meaning of Symbols

  • dB = small magnetic field contribution
  • I = current
  • dl = small conductor length
  • r = distance to observation point
  • θ = angle between dl and r
  • μ₀ = permeability of free space
μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

Circular Coil

At the center of a single circular loop:

B = μ₀I / 2R

For N turns:

B = μ₀NI / 2R
↻ I ┌───────────┐ / \ | ● | \ / └───────────┘ R B at the center is perpendicular to the plane of the loop.

Worked Example

A 50-turn coil carries 2 A and has radius 0.10 m.

B = μ₀NI/2R

B = [(4π × 10⁻⁷)(50)(2)]/[2(0.10)]

B ≈ 6.28 × 10⁻⁴ T
Biot–Savart is especially useful for calculating fields from particular current geometries such as loops and arcs.
Exam Practice
  1. State Biot–Savart law.
  2. Write its vector form.
  3. Explain every symbol.
  4. Derive the field of a long straight wire.
  5. Derive the field at the center of a circular coil.

4. 🔄 AMPÈRE'S LAW

Statement

Ampère's law relates the circulation of the magnetic field around a closed path to the current enclosed by that path.

∮ B⃗ · dl⃗ = μ₀Ienclosed

Amperian Loop

An Amperian loop is an imaginary closed path chosen to apply Ampère's law. The best path is one that takes advantage of symmetry.

↻ ↻ ↻ ┌─────────┐ / \ | I ↑ | \ / └─────────┘ Amperian loop

Long Straight Wire Derivation

∮B⃗·dl⃗ = μ₀I

Because B has the same magnitude around a circular path:

B∮dl = μ₀I

The circumference is 2πr:

B(2πr) = μ₀I
Therefore:

B = μ₀I / 2πr

Procedure for Ampère Problems

  1. Identify the symmetry.
  2. Choose an Amperian path.
  3. Write Ampère's law.
  4. Determine B along the path.
  5. Evaluate the integral.
  6. Solve for the unknown.
Do not automatically use Ampère's law for every magnetic field problem. It is most convenient when symmetry makes the integral simple.
Exam Practice
  1. State Ampère's law.
  2. What is an Amperian loop?
  3. Why is symmetry important?
  4. Derive B = μ₀I/2πr.
  5. Calculate B using Ampère's law.

5. 🔌 FORCE ON A CURRENT-CARRYING WIRE

Definition

A current-carrying conductor placed in an external magnetic field experiences a magnetic force.

F = BIL sinθ

Vector Form

F⃗ = I(L⃗ × B⃗)

Maximum Force

When θ = 90°:

F = BIL

Zero Force

When the wire is parallel to B:

θ = 0° → F = 0
B → → → → → → → ↑ F │ │ │ I │ │ Current-carrying wire

Force Between Parallel Wires

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂ / 2πr

Parallel currents in the same direction attract. Parallel currents in opposite directions repel.

Same direction: I₁ ↑ ↑ I₂ │ │ │ │ │ │ ATTRACT Opposite direction: I₁ ↑ ↓ I₂ │ │ │ │ │ │ REPEL

Worked Example

A 0.50 m wire carries 4 A in a 0.8 T field at 90°.

F = BIL

F = (0.8)(4)(0.50)

F = 1.6 N
Exam Practice
  1. State F = BIL sinθ.
  2. When is force maximum?
  3. When is force zero?
  4. Calculate the force on a conductor.
  5. State the force per unit length between two parallel wires.
  6. Explain attraction and repulsion of parallel currents.

6. 🌐 MAGNETIC FLUX

Definition

Magnetic flux is a measure of the magnetic field passing through a given surface.

Φ = BA cosθ

Here θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal (perpendicular line) to the surface.

Unit of Φ = weber (Wb)

Important Cases

Maximum flux:

θ = 0° → Φ = BA

Zero flux:

θ = 90° → Φ = 0

Area Vector

The area vector is perpendicular to the surface. This is why the angle in Φ = BA cosθ is measured from the normal rather than from the plane itself.

B → → → → → ┌───────────┐ │ SURFACE │ │ ↑ │ │ │ A │ └───────────┘ ↑ normal

Worked Example

B = 0.5 T, A = 2 m² and θ = 60°.

Φ = BA cos60°

Φ = (0.5)(2)(0.5)

Φ = 0.5 Wb
Exam Practice
  1. Define magnetic flux.
  2. State its SI unit.
  3. Write Φ = BA cosθ.
  4. Explain the meaning of the angle.
  5. Calculate magnetic flux through a surface.

7. ⚛️ MOTION OF CHARGED PARTICLES

Charge Moving Perpendicular to B

If a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field with its velocity perpendicular to B, the magnetic force continuously changes its direction of motion.

The speed remains constant in an ideal uniform magnetic field because the magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity.

Centripetal Force

qvB = mv²/r
Therefore:
r = mv/qB

Meaning

  • Greater mass → larger radius.
  • Greater velocity → larger radius.
  • Greater magnetic field → smaller radius.
  • Greater charge magnitude → smaller radius.

Helical Motion

If the particle has both a velocity component parallel to B and another component perpendicular to B, the particle follows a helical path.

B ↑ │ ↗ │ ↗ ↗ │ ↗ ●───────────● ↘ │ ↘ │ helical path
Exam Practice

  2. Derive r = mv/qB.
  3. Explain why the speed remains constant.
  4. What is helical motion?
  5. Calculate the radius of a particle's path.

8. 🔵 CIRCULAR MOTION IN A MAGNETIC FIELD

Radius

r = mv/qB

Angular Velocity

ω = qB/m

Strictly, for direction-independent scalar formulas, use the magnitude |q| when discussing frequency or angular frequency.

ω = |q|B/m

Period

T = 2πm/|q|B

Frequency

f = |q|B/2πm

Cyclotron Idea

A cyclotron uses a magnetic field to bend charged particles into circular paths while an alternating electric field accelerates them.

_________ / \ | ↻ ↻ | | ↻ ↻ | | ↻ ↻ | \___________/ Charged particle follows circular paths.
The period is independent of the particle's speed in the non-relativistic uniform-field model.

9. ✋ RIGHT-HAND RULES

Why They Matter

Right-hand rules help determine the direction of magnetic fields and magnetic forces.

Current-Carrying Straight Wire

Point your right thumb in the direction of conventional current. Your curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field around the wire.

↺ ↺ ↺ ↺ ↑ ↺ ↺ │ I ↺ ↺ │ ↺ ↺ ↺ ↺ Thumb = current Curled fingers = magnetic field

Force on Positive Charge

Use the cross product:

v⃗ × B⃗ = direction of F⃗

For a negative charge, reverse the resulting direction.

Three Things to Remember

  • Conventional current direction is used for ordinary current rules.
  • Electron motion is opposite to conventional current.
  • Negative charge reverses the force direction.
Exam Practice

  2. Determine the field direction around a wire.

10. 📐 UNIT VECTORS

Three Basic Directions

î = x-direction
ĵ = y-direction
k̂ = z-direction
z ↑ │ │ │ O────────→ x / / y î → x ĵ → y k̂ → z

Cross Products

î × ĵ = k̂
ĵ × k̂ = î
k̂ × î = ĵ

Reverse order gives a negative result:

ĵ × î = -k̂
k̂ × ĵ = -î
î × k̂ = -ĵ

Example

Calculate:

(2î) × (3ĵ)

Multiply the numbers:

2 × 3 = 6

Then:

î × ĵ = k̂
Answer = 6k̂

Magnetic Force Using Vectors

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗)

This is especially important when velocity and magnetic field are given using î, ĵ and k̂.

Memory: i → j → k → i is positive. Reverse direction is negative.
Exam Practice

  2. State î × ĵ.
  3. State ĵ × î.
  4. Calculate a vector cross product.
  5. Use vectors to calculate magnetic force.

11. ⚡ ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION

Definition

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an induced emf when the magnetic flux linking a circuit changes.

How Flux Can Change

  • Move a magnet toward or away from a coil.
  • Move the coil through a magnetic field.
  • Change the magnetic field strength.
  • Change the area of the coil.
  • Rotate the coil.
N → → → ┌─────────┐ │ COIL │ │ ↻ │ └─────────┘ Changing magnetic flux ↓ Induced emf ↓ Possible induced current

Faraday's Law

ε = -N dΦ/dt

The greater the rate of change of flux and the greater the number of turns, the greater the induced emf.

Motional EMF

For a straight conductor of length L moving with speed v perpendicular to a magnetic field:

ε = BLv

A 0.5 m conductor moves at 4 m/s perpendicular to a 2 T field.

ε = BLv

ε = (2)(0.5)(4)

ε = 4 V
Exam Practice
  1. Define electromagnetic induction.
  2. State two ways magnetic flux can change.
  3. State Faraday's law.
  4. Explain motional emf.
  5. Calculate induced emf.

12. 📜 FARADAY'S LAW

Statement

Faraday's law states that the magnitude of induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

ε = -N dΦ/dt

Average Form

ε = -N(ΔΦ/Δt)

If only magnitude is required:

|ε| = NΔΦ/Δt

Flux Linkage

If a coil has N turns and each turn experiences flux Φ, the flux linkage is NΦ.

Worked Example

A 100-turn coil experiences a change in flux of 0.02 Wb in 0.5 s. Find the magnitude of induced emf.

|ε| = NΔΦ/Δt

|ε| = (100)(0.02)/0.5

|ε| = 4 V
The negative sign in Faraday's law is connected to Lenz's law. It represents opposition to the change in flux.
Exam Practice
  1. State Faraday's law.
  2. Explain flux linkage.
  3. Explain the negative sign.
  4. Calculate average induced emf.
  5. Calculate the number of turns when emf is known.

13. 🔄 LENZ'S LAW

Statement

Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced current is such that the magnetic effect produced by the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it.

ε = -N dΦ/dt

The negative sign represents this opposition.

Magnet Approaching a Coil

N │ │ → → → → ┌─────────┐ │ │ COIL │ │ └─────────┘ MAGNET Flux is increasing. The coil produces a magnetic effect that opposes the increase.

Magnet Moving Away

If the magnet moves away, the magnetic flux decreases. The induced current produces a magnetic field that tends to oppose that decrease.

Why Lenz's Law Is Important

It is a direct consequence of conservation of energy. The induced effect cannot simply reinforce the change without an energy source.

Simple memory: Lenz's law says the induced effect opposes the change.
Exam Practice
  1. State Lenz's law.
  2. Explain the negative sign in Faraday's law.
  3. Determine induced-current direction when a magnet approaches a coil.
  4. Determine induced-current direction when a magnet moves away.
  5. Explain the relationship between Lenz's law and conservation of energy.

14. 🌀 INDUCTANCE

Definition

Inductance is the property of an electrical circuit that opposes changes in current by producing an induced emf.

Self-Inductance

ε = -L di/dt

L is the self-inductance and its SI unit is the henry (H).

Energy Stored in an Inductor

U = ½LI²

Worked Example

An inductor has L = 2 H and carries I = 3 A. Find the stored energy.

U = ½LI²

U = ½(2)(3²)

U = 1 × 9

U = 9 J

Mutual Inductance

Mutual inductance occurs when a changing current in one circuit produces an induced emf in another nearby circuit.

ε₂ = -M di₁/dt

M is the mutual inductance.

Inductor in a Solenoid

For an ideal long air-core solenoid:

L = μ₀N²A/l

where N is number of turns, A is cross-sectional area and l is length.

15. 🔌 AC CIRCUITS

Definition

Alternating current is an electric current whose magnitude and direction vary periodically with time.

Sinusoidal Voltage

v = V₀ sin(ωt)

V₀ is the peak voltage and ω is angular frequency.

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

RMS Voltage

Vrms = V₀/√2

RMS Current

Irms = I₀/√2

AC Resistor

For a pure resistor:

V = IR

Voltage and current are in phase.

Inductive Reactance

XL = ωL

Capacitive Reactance

XC = 1/ωC

Impedance in a Series RLC Circuit

Z = √[R² + (XL - XC)²]

RMS Current

Irms = Vrms/Z

Worked Example

An AC source has peak voltage 100 V. Find its RMS voltage.

Vrms = V₀/√2

Vrms = 100/√2

Vrms ≈ 70.7 V
Important: Do not confuse peak value with RMS value. For a sinusoidal waveform, RMS = peak/√2.
Exam Practice

  2. Write the equation for sinusoidal voltage.
  3. Define RMS voltage.
  4. Calculate RMS voltage from peak voltage.
  5. Define inductive reactance.
  6. Define capacitive reactance.
