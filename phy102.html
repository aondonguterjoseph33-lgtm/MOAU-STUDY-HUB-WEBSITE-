🧮 PHY 102 Formula Sheet

1. Magnetic Force on a Charge

F = qvB sinθ

Symbols: F = force, q = charge, v = velocity, B = magnetic field.

SI unit: Newton (N)

Use when: a charged particle moves through a magnetic field.

2. Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

I = current, r = distance from wire.

μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

3. Biot–Savart Law

dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl sinθ/r²)

Used to calculate the magnetic field produced by a small current element.

4. Ampère's Law

∮ B · dl = μ₀Ienclosed

Useful when the magnetic field has a high degree of symmetry.

5. Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

F = BIL sinθ

For perpendicular field: F = BIL

6. Magnetic Flux

Φ = BA cosθ

Φ = magnetic flux, A = area.

SI unit: Weber (Wb)

7. Radius of Charged Particle

r = mv / |q|B

8. Angular Frequency

ω = |q|B/m

9. Period of Circular Motion

T = 2πm / |q|B

10. Faraday's Law

ε = -N ΔΦ/Δt

11. Induced EMF

ε = -N dΦ/dt

12. Inductor

ε = -L di/dt

13. Energy Stored in an Inductor

U = ½LI²

14. Inductance of a Solenoid

L = μ₀N²A/l

15. AC Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

16. RMS Voltage

Vrms = V₀/√2

17. RMS Current

Irms = I₀/√2

18. Inductive Reactance

XL = ωL

19. Capacitive Reactance

XC = 1/(ωC)

20. Series RLC Impedance

Z = √[R² + (XL - XC)²]

📝 PHY 102 Exam Questions

Question 1: A charge of 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C moves with velocity 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 1.5 T. Calculate the magnetic force.

Formula: F = qvB

F = (3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2 × 10⁶)(1.5)

F = 9.6 × 10⁻¹³ N

Question 2: State Biot–Savart law.

Biot–Savart law states that the magnetic field due to a current element is proportional to the current, length element and sinθ, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the element.

dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl sinθ/r²)

Question 3: Calculate the magnetic field 0.2 m from a long straight wire carrying 5 A.

B = μ₀I / 2πr

B = [(4π × 10⁻⁷)(5)] / [2π(0.2)]

B = 5 × 10⁻⁶ T

Question 4: State Ampère's law.

∮B · dl = μ₀Ienclosed

Question 5: A wire of length 0.5 m carries a current of 4 A in a magnetic field of 0.8 T perpendicular to the wire. Find the force.

F = BIL

F = (0.8)(4)(0.5)

F = 1.6 N

Question 6: What happens to a charged particle moving parallel to a magnetic field?

Since θ = 0°, sin0° = 0. Therefore the magnetic force is zero and the particle is not deflected by the magnetic field.

Question 7: Define magnetic flux and give its formula.

Magnetic flux is a measure of the magnetic field passing through a surface.

Φ = BA cosθ

SI unit: Weber (Wb).

Question 8: State Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction.

The induced emf in a circuit is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

ε = -NΔΦ/Δt

Question 9: What does the negative sign in Faraday's law represent?

The negative sign represents Lenz's law: the induced effect opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it.

Question 10: Calculate the RMS voltage of an AC supply whose maximum voltage is 240 V.

Vrms = V₀/√2

Vrms = 240/√2

Vrms ≈ 169.7 V

1. ⚡ Magnetic Force

Definition

Magnetic force is the force experienced by a charged particle moving through a magnetic field.

F = qvB sinθ

Where:

  • F = magnetic force in newtons (N)
  • q = charge in coulombs (C)
  • v = velocity in m/s
  • B = magnetic field in tesla (T)
  • θ = angle between velocity and magnetic field

Vector Formula

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗)
B ↑ | | +q → v Magnetic force F is perpendicular to both v and B.

Important Cases

If θ = 90°:

F = qvB

If θ = 0° or 180°:

F = 0

Worked Example

Question:

A charge of 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C moves with velocity 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 1.5 T. Calculate the magnetic force.

Solution:

Since the charge moves perpendicular to the field:

θ = 90°

F = qvB

F = (3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2 × 10⁶)(1.5)

F = 9.6 × 10⁻¹³ N

Answer: 9.6 × 10⁻¹³ N

Exam Tip: Always check the angle before using F = qvB. The complete formula is F = qvB sinθ.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Define magnetic force.
  2. State the formula for magnetic force on a moving charge.
  3. When is magnetic force maximum?
  4. When is magnetic force zero?
  5. Calculate the force on a charge moving perpendicular to a magnetic field.

2. 🧲 Magnetic Field

Definition

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic forces can be experienced.

B = F / (qv sinθ)

The SI unit of magnetic field is the tesla (T).

Magnetic Field Lines

Magnetic field lines show the direction and relative strength of a magnetic field. Outside a bar magnet, field lines go from the north pole to the south pole.

N ─────────────→ S ↗ ↘ ↗ ↘ ↖ ↙ ↖ ↙ Field lines indicate the magnetic field direction.

Important Properties

  • Magnetic field lines never cross each other.
  • Closer lines represent a stronger field.
  • The field direction is along the tangent to a field line.
  • The SI unit of B is tesla.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Define magnetic field.
  2. State the SI unit of magnetic field.
  3. What do magnetic field lines represent?
  4. State three properties of magnetic field lines.

3. 🧮 Biot–Savart Law

The Biot–Savart law is used to calculate the magnetic field produced by a small element of current.

dB = (μ₀ / 4π) × (I dl sinθ / r²)

Where:

  • μ₀ = permeability of free space
  • I = current
  • dl = small length element
  • r = distance from the current element
  • θ = angle between dl and r
μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / (2πr)

Centre of a Circular Loop

B = μ₀I / (2R)

For N turns:

B = μ₀NI / (2R)
Remember: Biot–Savart law is especially useful when the current geometry does not have enough symmetry for a simple Ampère's law calculation.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. State the Biot–Savart law.
  2. Define permeability of free space.
  3. Derive the magnetic field due to a long straight wire.
  4. Calculate the field at the center of a circular current loop.

4. 🔄 Ampère's Law

Ampère's law relates the circulation of the magnetic field around a closed path to the current enclosed by that path.

∮ B⃗ · dl⃗ = μ₀Ienclosed

How to Solve Ampère's Law Questions

  1. Identify the symmetry of the system.
  2. Choose a suitable Amperian loop.
  3. Determine where B is constant.
  4. Evaluate the line integral.
  5. Set it equal to μ₀I.
  6. Solve for B.

Long Straight Wire

B(2πr) = μ₀I
Therefore:
B = μ₀I / (2πr)
B → → → ┌───────────┐ ↗ │ │ ↘ ↑ │ I ↑ │ ↓ ↖ │ │ ↙ └───────────┘ Circular Amperian path
Key idea: In Ampère's law, symmetry helps you take B outside the integral.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. State Ampère's law.
  2. Explain what an Amperian loop is.
  3. Why is symmetry important in Ampère's law?
  4. Use Ampère's law to derive the field around a long straight wire.

5. 🔌 Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force.

F = BIL sinθ

Where:

  • B = magnetic field
  • I = current
  • L = length of conductor
  • θ = angle between wire and magnetic field

For a perpendicular wire:

F = BIL
B → → → → → → → ↑ F | | I | ↓ Current-carrying wire
Possible Exam Questions
  1. State the formula for force on a current-carrying wire.
  2. What happens when the conductor is parallel to the field?
  3. Calculate the force on a wire carrying a given current.

6. 🌐 Magnetic Flux

Magnetic flux measures the amount of magnetic field passing through a surface.

Φ = BA cosθ

Where:

  • Φ = magnetic flux
  • B = magnetic field
  • A = area
  • θ = angle between B and the normal to the surface
SI unit of magnetic flux = weber (Wb)

Maximum flux occurs when θ = 0°.

Φmax = BA
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Define magnetic flux.
  2. State the formula for magnetic flux.
  3. State the SI unit of magnetic flux.
  4. Calculate flux through a given surface.

7. ⚛️ Motion of Charged Particles

A charged particle moving through a magnetic field can experience a force that changes the direction of its motion.

If the velocity is perpendicular to the magnetic field, the magnetic force acts as a centripetal force.

qvB = mv²/r

Therefore:

r = mv / (qB)

The particle follows a circular path.

v → ●──────── ↗ ↘ ● ● ↖ ↙ ●───────● Charged particle circular path
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Describe the motion of a charged particle in a uniform magnetic field.
  2. Derive r = mv/qB.
  3. Calculate the radius of a charged particle's path.

8. 🔵 Circular Motion in a Magnetic Field

r = mv / (qB)

Angular Frequency

ω = qB / m

Period

T = 2πm / (qB)

Frequency

f = qB / (2πm)
The radius depends on velocity, but the period of circular motion in a uniform magnetic field does not depend on velocity in the non-relativistic case.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Derive the radius of circular motion.
  2. Calculate the period of a charged particle.
  3. Calculate the frequency of circular motion.

9. ✋ Right-Hand Rules

Right-hand rules help determine the direction of magnetic fields, forces, and motion involving currents and moving charges.

For a Positive Charge

Point your fingers in the direction of velocity v, curl them toward the magnetic field B. Your thumb gives the direction of magnetic force.

For a Negative Charge

The force direction is opposite to the direction obtained for a positive charge.

THUMB ↑ │ F │ ✋ RIGHT HAND / / / v B Velocity × Magnetic field determines force direction.
Exam Tip: Always check whether the charge is positive or negative. Electrons require the opposite force direction.

10. 📐 Unit Vectors in Magnetism

Unit vectors describe directions in three-dimensional space.

î = x-direction
ĵ = y-direction
k̂ = z-direction

Important Cross Products

î × ĵ = k̂
ĵ × k̂ = î
k̂ × î = ĵ

Reversing the order changes the sign:

ĵ × î = -k̂
k̂ × ĵ = -î
î × k̂ = -ĵ
Remember the cycle: i → j → k → i gives positive results. The reverse direction gives negative results.
Practice

Calculate:

(2î) × (3ĵ)

Answer:

6k̂

11. ⚡ Electromagnetic Induction

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an emf when the magnetic flux through a circuit changes.

A change in magnetic field, area, or orientation can change the magnetic flux.

ε = - dΦ/dt

For N turns:

ε = -N dΦ/dt
MAGNET N → → → ┌───────────┐ │ COIL │ │ ↻ │ └───────────┘ Changing magnetic flux produces induced emf.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Define electromagnetic induction.
  2. What causes induced emf?
  3. State Faraday's law.
  4. State Lenz's law.

12. 📜 Faraday's Law

Faraday's law states that the induced emf in a circuit is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

ε = -N dΦ/dt

The negative sign represents Lenz's law: the induced effect opposes the change producing it.

Average Form

ε = -N(ΔΦ/Δt)
Example:

A coil has 100 turns. The magnetic flux changes by 0.02 Wb in 0.5 s. Find the magnitude of the average induced emf.

Use:

|ε| = NΔΦ/Δt

|ε| = (100 × 0.02)/0.5

|ε| = 4 V

Answer: 4 V

13. 🔄 Lenz's Law

Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced current is such that its magnetic effect opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it.

Simple meaning: Nature opposes the change.

For example, if magnetic flux through a coil is increasing, the induced magnetic field acts in a direction that opposes that increase.

Possible Exam Questions
  1. State Lenz's law.
  2. Explain the meaning of the negative sign in Faraday's law.
  3. Determine the direction of an induced current using Lenz's law.

14. 🌀 Inductance

Inductance is the property of a circuit that opposes changes in current.

Self-Inductance

ε = -L di/dt

Where L is the self-inductance.

Unit of inductance = henry (H)

Energy Stored in an Inductor

U = ½LI²
Example:

An inductor of 2 H carries a current of 3 A. Calculate the energy stored.

U = ½LI²

U = ½(2)(3²)

U = 9 J

Answer: 9 J

15. 🔌 AC Circuits

Alternating current is an electric current that changes magnitude and direction periodically.

Instantaneous AC Voltage

V = V₀ sin(ωt)

RMS Voltage

Vrms = V₀ / √2

RMS Current

Irms = I₀ / √2

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

Where f is frequency in hertz.

Remember: RMS values are commonly used to describe the effective voltage and current in AC circuits.
Possible Exam Questions
  1. Define alternating current.
  2. Write the equation for sinusoidal AC voltage.
  3. Define RMS voltage.
  4. Calculate RMS voltage from peak voltage.
  5. Calculate angular frequency from frequency.

1. ⚡ MAGNETIC FORCE

Definition

Magnetic force is the force exerted by a magnetic field on a moving charged particle or a current-carrying conductor.

Force on a Moving Charge

F = qvB sinθ

Where q is charge, v is velocity, B is magnetic field and θ is the angle between velocity and magnetic field.

Vector Form

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗)
B ↑ │ │ ●────────→ v / / F F is perpendicular to v and B

Special Cases

1. θ = 90°

F = qvB

The force is maximum.

2. θ = 0°

F = 0

The particle is moving parallel to the field.

3. θ = 180°

F = 0

The particle is moving opposite to the field.

Direction

For a positive charge, use the right-hand rule. For a negative charge, the direction is opposite.

Worked Example

A charge of 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C moves at 2 × 10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 1.5 T magnetic field. Find the force.

F = qvB

F = (3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹)(2 × 10⁶)(1.5)

F = 9.6 × 10⁻¹³ N

Important Facts

  • Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity.
  • A magnetic field does no work on a moving charged particle.
  • Force increases when q, v or B increases.
  • Maximum force occurs at 90°.
Common mistake: Do not forget sinθ. Use F = qvB only when the motion is perpendicular to the magnetic field.
Exam Practice
  1. Define magnetic force.
  2. State the magnetic force equation.
  3. When is magnetic force maximum?
  4. When is magnetic force zero?
  5. Calculate the force on a moving charge.
  6. Explain the direction of force on a negative charge.

2. 🧲 MAGNETIC FIELD

Definition

A magnetic field is the region around a magnet, current-carrying conductor, or moving charge where magnetic effects can be experienced.

Unit of magnetic field = Tesla (T)

Magnetic Field Lines

→ → → → → → → ↗ ↘ ↗ ↘ N S ↘ ↗ ↘ ↗ → → → → → → → BAR MAGNET

Properties

  • Outside a magnet, lines go from N to S.
  • They form closed loops.
  • They never cross.
  • Closer lines indicate stronger fields.
  • The tangent gives field direction.

Field Due to a Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

The field increases with current and decreases with distance.

μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Worked Example

A wire carries 10 A. Find B at 0.20 m.

B = μ₀I / 2πr

B = [(4π × 10⁻⁷)(10)] / [(2π)(0.20)]

B = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ T
Remember: Doubling I doubles B. Doubling r halves B.
Exam Practice
  1. Define magnetic field.
  2. State its SI unit.
  3. List five properties of magnetic field lines.
  4. Derive B = μ₀I/2πr.
  5. Calculate the field around a straight wire.

3. 🧮 BIOT–SAVART LAW

Meaning

The Biot–Savart law determines the magnetic field contribution produced by a small current element.

dB = (μ₀/4π)(I dl sinθ/r²)

Vector Form

dB⃗ = (μ₀/4π)[I(dl⃗ × r̂)/r²]

Meaning of Symbols

  • dB = small magnetic field contribution
  • I = current
  • dl = small conductor length
  • r = distance to observation point
  • θ = angle between dl and r
  • μ₀ = permeability of free space
μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A

Long Straight Wire

B = μ₀I / 2πr

Circular Coil

At the center of a single circular loop:

B = μ₀I / 2R

For N turns:

B = μ₀NI / 2R
↻ I ┌───────────┐ / \ | ● | \ / └───────────┘ R B at the center is perpendicular to the plane of the loop.

Worked Example

A 50-turn coil carries 2 A and has radius 0.10 m.

B = μ₀NI/2R

B = [(4π × 10⁻⁷)(50)(2)]/[2(0.10)]

B ≈ 6.28 × 10⁻⁴ T
Biot–Savart is especially useful for calculating fields from particular current geometries such as loops and arcs.

  3. Explain every symbol.
  4. Derive the field of a long straight wire.
  5. Derive the field at the center of a circular coil.

4. 🔄 AMPÈRE'S LAW

Statement

Ampère's law relates the circulation of the magnetic field around a closed path to the current enclosed by that path.

∮ B⃗ · dl⃗ = μ₀Ienclosed

Amperian Loop

An Amperian loop is an imaginary closed path chosen to apply Ampère's law. The best path is one that takes advantage of symmetry.

↻ ↻ ↻ ┌─────────┐ / \ | I ↑ | \ / └─────────┘ Amperian loop

Long Straight Wire Derivation

∮B⃗·dl⃗ = μ₀I

Because B has the same magnitude around a circular path:

B∮dl = μ₀I

The circumference is 2πr:

B(2πr) = μ₀I
Therefore:

B = μ₀I / 2πr

Procedure for Ampère Problems

  1. Identify the symmetry.
  2. Choose an Amperian path.
  3. Write Ampère's law.
  4. Determine B along the path.
  5. Evaluate the integral.
  6. Solve for the unknown.
Do not automatically use Ampère's law for every magnetic field problem. It is most convenient when symmetry makes the integral simple.

  2. What is an Amperian loop?
  3. Why is symmetry important?

  5. Calculate B using Ampère's law.

5. 🔌 FORCE ON A CURRENT-CARRYING WIRE

Definition

A current-carrying conductor placed in an external magnetic field experiences a magnetic force.

F = BIL sinθ

Vector Form

F⃗ = I(L⃗ × B⃗)

Maximum Force

When θ = 90°:

F = BIL

Zero Force

When the wire is parallel to B:

θ = 0° → F = 0
B → → → → → → → ↑ F │ │ │ I │ │ Current-carrying wire

Force Between Parallel Wires

F/L = μ₀I₁I₂ / 2πr

Parallel currents in the same direction attract. Parallel currents in opposite directions repel.

Same direction: I₁ ↑ ↑ I₂ │ │ │ │ │ │ ATTRACT Opposite direction: I₁ ↑ ↓ I₂ │ │ │ │ │ │ REPEL

Worked Example

A 0.50 m wire carries 4 A in a 0.8 T field at 90°.

F = BIL

F = (0.8)(4)(0.50)

F = 1.6 N

6. 🌐 MAGNETIC FLUX

Definition

Magnetic flux is a measure of the magnetic field passing through a given surface.

Φ = BA cosθ

Here θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the normal (perpendicular line) to the surface.

Unit of Φ = weber (Wb)

Important Cases

Maximum flux:

θ = 0° → Φ = BA

Zero flux:

θ = 90° → Φ = 0

Area Vector

The area vector is perpendicular to the surface. This is why the angle in Φ = BA cosθ is measured from the normal rather than from the plane itself.

B → → → → → ┌───────────┐ │ SURFACE │ │ ↑ │ │ │ A │ └───────────┘ ↑ normal

Worked Example

B = 0.5 T, A = 2 m² and θ = 60°.

Φ = BA cos60°

Φ = (0.5)(2)(0.5)

Φ = 0.5 Wb

7. ⚛️ MOTION OF CHARGED PARTICLES

Charge Moving Perpendicular to B

If a charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field with its velocity perpendicular to B, the magnetic force continuously changes its direction of motion.

The speed remains constant in an ideal uniform magnetic field because the magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity.

Centripetal Force

qvB = mv²/r
Therefore:
r = mv/qB

Meaning

  • Greater mass → larger radius.
  • Greater velocity → larger radius.
  • Greater magnetic field → smaller radius.
  • Greater charge magnitude → smaller radius.

Helical Motion

If the particle has both a velocity component parallel to B and another component perpendicular to B, the particle follows a helical path.

B ↑ │ ↗ │ ↗ ↗ │ ↗ ●───────────● ↘ │ ↘ │ helical path

8. 🔵 CIRCULAR MOTION IN A MAGNETIC FIELD

Radius

Angular Velocity

ω = |q|B/m

Period

T = 2πm/|q|B

Frequency

Cyclotron Idea

A cyclotron uses a magnetic field to bend charged particles into circular paths while an alternating electric field accelerates them.

The period is independent of the particle's speed in the non-relativistic uniform-field model.

9. ✋ RIGHT-HAND RULES

Why They Matter

Right-hand rules help determine the direction of magnetic fields and magnetic forces.

Current-Carrying Straight Wire

Point your right thumb in the direction of conventional current. Your curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field around the wire.

↺ ↺ ↺ ↺ ↑ ↺ ↺ │ I ↺ ↺ │ ↺ ↺ ↺ ↺ Thumb = current Curled fingers = magnetic field

Force on Positive Charge

Use the cross product:

v⃗ × B⃗ = direction of F⃗

For a negative charge, reverse the resulting direction.

Three Things to Remember

  • Conventional current direction is used for ordinary current rules.
  • Electron motion is opposite to conventional current.
  • Negative charge reverses the force direction.

10. 📐 UNIT VECTORS

Three Basic Directions

î = x-direction
ĵ = y-direction
k̂ = z-direction
z ↑ │ │ │ O────────→ x / / y î → x ĵ → y k̂ → z

Cross Products

î × ĵ = k̂
ĵ × k̂ = î
k̂ × î = ĵ

Reverse order gives a negative result:

ĵ × î = -k̂
k̂ × ĵ = -î
î × k̂ = -ĵ

Example

Calculate:

(2î) × (3ĵ)

Multiply the numbers:

2 × 3 = 6

Then:

î × ĵ = k̂
Answer = 6k̂

Magnetic Force Using Vectors

F⃗ = q(v⃗ × B⃗)

This is especially important when velocity and magnetic field are given using î, ĵ and k̂.

Memory: i → j → k → i is positive. Reverse direction is negative.

11. ⚡ ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION

Definition

Electromagnetic induction is the production of an induced emf when the magnetic flux linking a circuit changes.

How Flux Can Change

  • Move a magnet toward or away from a coil.
  • Move the coil through a magnetic field.
  • Change the magnetic field strength.
  • Change the area of the coil.
  • Rotate the coil.
N → → → ┌─────────┐ │ COIL │ │ ↻ │ └─────────┘ Changing magnetic flux ↓ Induced emf ↓ Possible induced current

Faraday's Law

ε = -N dΦ/dt

The greater the rate of change of flux and the greater the number of turns, the greater the induced emf.

Motional EMF

For a straight conductor of length L moving with speed v perpendicular to a magnetic field:

ε = BLv

A 0.5 m conductor moves at 4 m/s perpendicular to a 2 T field.

ε = BLv

ε = (2)(0.5)(4)

ε = 4 V

12. 📜 FARADAY'S LAW

Statement

Faraday's law states that the magnitude of induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage.

ε = -N dΦ/dt

Average Form

ε = -N(ΔΦ/Δt)

If only magnitude is required:

|ε| = NΔΦ/Δt

Flux Linkage

If a coil has N turns and each turn experiences flux Φ, the flux linkage is NΦ.

Worked Example

A 100-turn coil experiences a change in flux of 0.02 Wb in 0.5 s. Find the magnitude of induced emf.

|ε| = NΔΦ/Δt

|ε| = (100)(0.02)/0.5

|ε| = 4 V
The negative sign in Faraday's law is connected to Lenz's law. It represents opposition to the change in flux.

13. 🔄 LENZ'S LAW

Statement

Lenz's law states that the direction of an induced current is such that the magnetic effect produced by the induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux that caused it.

ε = -N dΦ/dt

The negative sign represents this opposition.

Magnet Approaching a Coil

N │ │ → → → → ┌─────────┐ │ │ COIL │ │ └─────────┘ MAGNET Flux is increasing. The coil produces a magnetic effect that opposes the increase.

Magnet Moving Away

If the magnet moves away, the magnetic flux decreases. The induced current produces a magnetic field that tends to oppose that decrease.

Why Lenz's Law Is Important

It is a direct consequence of conservation of energy. The induced effect cannot simply reinforce the change without an energy source.

Simple memory: Lenz's law says the induced effect opposes the change.

  4. Determine induced-current direction when a magnet moves away.

14. 🌀 INDUCTANCE

Definition

Inductance is the property of an electrical circuit that opposes changes in current by producing an induced emf.

Self-Inductance

ε = -L di/dt

L is the self-inductance and its SI unit is the henry (H).

Energy Stored in an Inductor

U = ½LI²

Worked Example

An inductor has L = 2 H and carries I = 3 A. Find the stored energy.

U = ½LI²

U = ½(2)(3²)

U = 1 × 9

U = 9 J

Mutual Inductance

Mutual inductance occurs when a changing current in one circuit produces an induced emf in another nearby circuit.

ε₂ = -M di₁/dt

M is the mutual inductance.

Inductor in a Solenoid

For an ideal long air-core solenoid:

L = μ₀N²A/l

where N is number of turns, A is cross-sectional area and l is length.

15. 🔌 AC CIRCUITS

Definition

Alternating current is an electric current whose magnitude and direction vary periodically with time.

Sinusoidal Voltage

v = V₀ sin(ωt)

V₀ is the peak voltage and ω is angular frequency.

Angular Frequency

ω = 2πf

RMS Voltage

Vrms = V₀/√2

RMS Current

Irms = I₀/√2

AC Resistor

For a pure resistor:

V = IR

Voltage and current are in phase.

Inductive Reactance

XL = ωL

Capacitive Reactance

XC = 1/ωC

Impedance in a Series RLC Circuit

Z = √[R² + (XL - XC)²]

RMS Current

Irms = Vrms/Z

Worked Example

An AC source has peak voltage 100 V. Find its RMS voltage.

Vrms = V₀/√2

Vrms = 100/√2

Vrms ≈ 70.7 V
Important: Do not confuse peak value with RMS value. For a sinusoidal waveform, RMS = peak/√2.
